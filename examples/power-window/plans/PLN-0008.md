## Scope

The window control software (SYE-0001, the components of CMP-0001) inside the customer's
front door module. The customer asked for a threat analysis and risk assessment of the
window control function at component level, on the customer's item definition (ITM-0001),
and owns the item, its concept and the vehicle: clauses 9 and 11 to 14 of ISO/SAE 21434
are the customer's. In scope here, as `scope.cybersecurity` of `project.yaml` says:
clause 6 for this project and clause 15 for the analysis.

## Approach

Assets are taken from the interfaces of the item definition that cross the boundary of
the window control software, and from what the software holds. Threat scenarios are
found per asset and property with STRIDE. Ratings follow `tara_method` above; the risk
value of a threat scenario is that of the highest impact it leads to. A risk to reduce
gives a cybersecurity goal, which goes to the customer for the cybersecurity concept;
a risk shared with the customer or retained gives a cybersecurity claim.
`python tools/traceability.py tara` shows the analysis.

## Criteria

The analysis is complete when every asset is threatened by a threat scenario, every
damage scenario is reached, every threat scenario has a treatment, and the goals and
claims are reviewed. A risk value above 2 is not retained without the decision of the
cybersecurity lead.

## Environment and tools

The items in this repository; `tools/traceability.py tara` and `clauses`.

## Responsibilities

Cybersecurity lead: this plan, the treatment decisions, the cybersecurity case.
Cybersecurity engineer: the analysis. Functional safety lead: the safety ratings.
Project lead: responsibilities and resources. Customer: the item definition, the
concept, everything in the vehicle.
