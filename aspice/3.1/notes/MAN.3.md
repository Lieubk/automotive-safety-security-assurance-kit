# MAN.3 Project Management

## Intent
Project management keeps a development project steerable. What has to be delivered is bounded, the work and the people, equipment and budget it needs are planned, and actual progress is compared with the plan often enough to react in time. An assessor looks less for a plan document than for evidence that scope, life cycle, estimates, activities, schedule and commitments fit together and are kept current. The process belongs to the VDA scope and has rating-consistency guidance.

## Outcomes
1. The boundaries of what the project has to deliver are stated.
2. It has been checked whether the project objectives can be reached with the resources at hand and under the given constraints.
3. The work to be done and the resources it requires have been sized and estimated.
4. The project knows its internal interfaces and those to neighbouring projects and organizational units, and keeps an eye on them.
5. Plans for carrying out the project exist, are followed and are kept up to date.
6. Progress is tracked and reported.
7. Missed objectives trigger corrective action, and problems found in the project are kept from coming back.

Interpretation hints:
- Activities, estimates, schedule and interfaces are expected to span engineering work as well as management and support work, not only design, coding and test.
- Work packages need a structure and a size that make progress measurable.
- Resources to estimate include staff, tooling and test equipment, communication infrastructure, hardware and material.
- Risk information (MAN.5) and quality criteria (SUP.1) can feed the estimates.
- Missing skills are normally closed by training.
- Management reviews at fixed intervals plus a closing review that collects good practices and lessons learned are the usual way to implement BP10.
- IDs whose prefix is not `MAN.3` come from cross-cutting chapters of the VDA guideline or from other processes.
- For BP9 the pair MAN.3.RC.14 / MAN.3.RC.15 is an interpretation of an ambiguous range and should be confirmed against the guideline.

## Automation notes
Assumed set-up: the project plan is a structured text file, work packages and schedule items are records with IDs (YAML, CSV or an issue-tracker export), status reports are generated or stored per period, baselines are tags.

Script (deterministic):
- The plan file contains the mandatory sections (scope, life cycle, estimates, resources, interfaces, reporting) and each is non-empty.
- Every work package has an owner, an estimate, a status and planned dates; every schedule item points to an existing work package; dependency links resolve and contain no cycle.
- Work packages exist for engineering, management and supporting processes (a tag per package, counted per category).
- Planned versus actual effort and dates per package; flag deviations beyond a threshold and packages with no status update for longer than the reporting interval.
- Each role named in the resource plan appears in the skill matrix; each skill gap has a training entry with a date.
- A status report exists for every reporting period; corrective actions have owner, due date and status; overdue actions are listed.
- Milestone dates in the schedule match the dates of the corresponding baseline tags.
- Cross-file consistency: milestones in plan, schedule and release plan carry identical names and dates; commitments in the interface list appear as schedule items.

LLM (semantic judgement):
- Does the scope statement agree with the requirements baseline and the customer agreement?
- Is the basis of an estimate plausible and is the method actually applied, or is the number unexplained?
- Does the narrative of a status report match the data (for example "on track" while key packages slip)?
- Draft the periodic status report and the deviation summary from repository data for the Project Manager to edit.

Human (accountability):
- The feasibility decision, the commitment to dates and scope, and acceptance of a re-plan.
- Negotiating interfaces and commitments with stakeholders, assigning people, deciding on corrective action and escalation.
